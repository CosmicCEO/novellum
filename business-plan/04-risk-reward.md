# 4. Risk and reward

Sahlman: *What can go wrong? Who bears it? What is the upside if it goes right?*

## Deal-killing risks

| Risk | How it kills | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| No CNS myeloid exposure after IV | Commercial product dies | Run CSF in parallel; stop IV if ratio is hopeless |
| Neuron/astrocyte transfection only | Wrong cell for TREM2 | Sorted microglia assays required |
| TREM2 biology is stage-wrong | PD without benefit | Early AD + plaque-associated microglia models |
| LNP innate immunity / liver | Un-dosable | Empty-particle control; cytokine panel; PEG strategy |
| Lipid FTO | Cannot manufacture or partner | Counsel letter before IND spend |
| Cash before the gate | Company expires mid-screen | Seed sized to one gate only |
| Crowded TREM2 clinic | Partners wait for human PD | Stay cheap until myeloid protein is shown |

## Reward if gates hit

- Seed holders own a delivery-validated TREM2 RNA asset in a genetically credentialed AD node.
- Series A becomes raisable on PD, not on slides.
- Partner path: large-cap AD franchise (Lilly, Biogen, Roche, AbbVie, Sanofi) buying optionality on a distinct modality.
- Downstream RNA payloads on the same particle are optional—not the raise thesis.

## Risk allocation

- Founders: time and early dilution.
- Seed: experiment risk, not Phase 3 risk.
- Grants: non-dilutive share of the same experiment.
- Series A: tox, CMC, and first-in-human risk only after the delivery gate.
