# Method — HBS venture plan

## Source of record

William A. Sahlman, “How to Write a Great Business Plan,” *Harvard Business Review*, July–August 1997.
Still the spine of HBS opportunity evaluation (Entrepreneurial Manager; Rock Center / New Venture Competition coaching; HBS Club plan outlines).

Sahlman’s point: investors do not buy a forecast. They buy a team facing a moving opportunity in a moving context, with risk that can be bounded.

## The four factors

### 1. People
Who are they? What have they done? Whom do they know? What is missing on the team, and how will it be filled? For Novellum this includes operators who have shipped sterile injectables and scientists who have shipped microglia or LNP data.

### 2. Opportunity
Who is the customer (patient + prescriber + payer)? How acute is the need? Is the product defined tightly enough to test? What is the economic engine (price, COGS, trial cost, time to cash)? What is the *minimum* experiment that can invalidate the opportunity?

### 3. Context
FDA/CBER gene-material and LNP precedents, TREM2 competitive set, BBB/mRNA delivery state of the art, NIH/NIA and foundation capital, lipid IP thicket, AD trial design norms after anti-amyloid approvals.

### 4. Risk and reward
Enumerate risks that can kill the company (delivery, biology window, innate immunity, FTO, cash). State who bears them. State the reward if gates hit (ownership, follow-on, partner path). Hide nothing.

## HBS 2020s overlay

From Launching Technology Ventures / Foundry-style practice:

- Write **hypotheses**, not slogans.
- Fund **experiments** that can fail in months, not years.
- Update the plan when data move. The files in `business-plan/` are living.
